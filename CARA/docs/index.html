3.7 — Communication Design

CARA integrates three distinct communication protocols, each chosen to satisfy specific functional and data requirements of its subsystems.

🔌

UART Serial — Jetson Nano ↔ Arduino

9600 baud via USB Serial. One-way signaling: Jetson decides, Arduino executes. Arduino reads with Serial.readStringUntil('\n'). Commands: FALL_DETECTED, STEER_LEFT, STEER_RIGHT, MOVE_FORWARD, SLOW_DOWN, STOP, TARGET_LOST, PAN_LEFT, PAN_RIGHT.

📡

I2C Bus — LCD + RTC

Shared two-wire bus (SDA/SCL on Arduino A4/A5). LCD at addr 0x27, DS3231 RTC at addr 0x68. Reduces GPIO usage — both peripherals on two wires. Scalable for additional I2C devices.

📱

GSM AT Commands — SIM76xx

SoftwareSerial on Arduino pins 2 & 3. Hayes AT command set: AT+CMGF=1 (SMS text mode), AT+CMGS (send message). 1000µF capacitor stabilises transmission current spikes. 4.0V regulated supply via LM2596 buck converter. Same command set works on SIM800L, used in a Sri Lanka deployment where 2G is still active.

Wi-Fi is intentionally excluded from all safety-critical communication paths as a deliberate design decision — ensuring independence from local network availability rather than reflecting a system constraint.

3.8 — Safety Design

Safety is a primary system constraint in CARA, not an auxiliary feature. The design is informed by ISO 13482:2014 (personal care robots), implemented across three layers:

Physical Collision Safety

6 × HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors provide continuous real-time distance monitoring. Emergency stop at 20cm threshold — non-overridable even during remote drive. Threshold selected based on maximum 0.3m/s operating speed and motor response latency.

Functional Safety — Fall Detection

2-second confirmation window required before a fall event is officially registered — prevents single-frame misclassification triggering alerts. 30-second cooldown enforced after each alert to prevent SMS flooding. Target: <2 false positives per hour under continuous operation.

Electrical & Power Safety

GSM module (SIM76xx series; SIM800L in a Sri Lanka deployment) powered via dedicated 4.0V buck converter (cannot use Arduino 5V rail — high current during TX bursts). 1000µF capacitor across the module's terminals stabilises transient spikes. LiPo includes integrated protection circuit preventing over-discharge. High-current and low-power circuits use separate power rails.

Staircase / Edge Safety Postponed → v1.1

Three TCRT5000 downward-facing IR sensors would detect loss of floor surface, emergency-stopping if 2+ sensors simultaneously show no floor reflection — preventing the robot from driving off a step edge or into a staircase. Proposed for v1.0 but postponed to v1.1 due to time constraints — not implemented or tested in the current build. Kept here for documentation continuity.

CARA v1.0 has not undergone formal ISO 13482 certification — it is a research prototype. Any real-world deployment in eldercare environments would require additional validation, extended user trials, and clinical safety assessments.
