Research & Background

A review of the current landscape in elderly care, assistive robotics, fall detection, GSM-based health alerting, and HRI — culminating in a clear identification of the research gap that CARA addresses.

2.1 — Ageing and the Elderly Care Crisis

Sri Lanka is experiencing one of the most rapid demographic transitions in South Asia. The proportion of people aged 60 and above increased from 12.4% in 2012 and is projected to reach nearly one quarter of the population by 2041.

According to recent census estimates, the elderly population increased from 12% in 2012 to approximately 18% in 2024 and is expected to reach 25% by 2040. The rising old-age dependency ratio means a smaller working population must support a growing elderly population, posing significant economic and social challenges.

This demographic shift is compounded by large-scale economic migration of working-age adults abroad, leaving elderly parents living alone in family homes without consistent daily supervision or immediate care access.

Older adults living alone face higher risks of falls, medication non-adherence, delayed medical response in emergencies, and social isolation — risks further exacerbated by unreliable internet connectivity, limited access to formal care services, and low digital literacy in rural and semi-urban settings.

2.2 — Assistive Robot Landscape

PARO, a robotic therapeutic seal, improves emotional well-being among elderly patients with dementia, but its $5,000+ cost and supervised operation limit deployment. Pepper supports elderly users through conversation and reminders but requires Wi-Fi, limiting use in connectivity-constrained settings. Care-O-Bot 4 enables autonomous navigation and task assistance but costs over $100,000 and remains a research prototype. ElliQ offers AI companion features at $250/month but requires continuous internet and is English-only.

2.3 — Fall Detection Methods

Wearable accelerometers provide accurate fall detection (95%+ sensitivity) but depend on continuous user compliance — a critical issue for cognitively impaired elderly users.

Vision-based systems using pose estimation achieve detection rates around 92% but raise privacy concerns and struggle with occlusions in real home environments. Systems using MediaPipe Pose Estimation have demonstrated ~95.8% accuracy extracting 33 key points from standard video streams without additional sensors.

Floor sensor networks can detect falls unobtrusively without wearables but require installation infrastructure and have limited spatial coverage.

CARA uses MediaPipe Pose on NVIDIA Jetson Nano — checking LEFT_HIP y >0.77, RIGHT_HIP y >0.77, and LEFT_SHOULDER y >0.55 simultaneously for 2 seconds before confirming a fall, minimising false positives while achieving high detection rates.

2.4 — Robot Navigation and Indoor Mobility

SLAM-based navigation systems using ROS (GMapping, RTAB-Map) achieve reliable indoor localization and path planning. Comparative evaluations reveal differences in mapping quality underscoring the importance of algorithm selection for low-cost deployments.

Hybrid frameworks such as RL-DWA integrate reinforcement learning with local planners to navigate toward and follow users in cluttered indoor environments. CARA implements a simpler but effective approach: 6 ultrasonic sensors providing 360° coverage, combined with PIR-triggered person following using HSV color band tracking.

2.5 — GSM and IoT-Based Health Alerting

IoT-based health monitoring systems combining vital sign sensing, fall detection, and GSM alerting deliver real-time SMS notifications without Wi-Fi — suitable for environments with limited internet access. GSM SMS can deliver reliable alerts even in rural areas where internet infrastructure is unavailable or unstable.

CARA's SIM76xx GSM module (SIM800L for a Sri Lanka deployment) uses Hayes AT command set (AT+CMGF=1 for text mode, AT+CMGS for transmission) and requires a dedicated regulated 4.0V supply separate from the Arduino 5V rail to handle transmission current bursts (stabilised by a 1000µF capacitor).

This approach enables CARA to function in Sri Lanka's rural connectivity landscape — where GSM cellular coverage is broadly available even where broadband internet is not.

2.6 — Human-Robot Interaction with Elderly Users

Research shows that perceived usefulness, ease of use, safety, reliability, and emotional connection strongly influence elderly acceptance of robotic assistants. Elderly users prefer robots requiring minimal interaction for core functions, supporting CARA's zero-interaction design philosophy.

Non-verbal communication cues — gestures, visual feedback (LED colours), and contextual LCD messages — significantly improve trust and engagement without demanding technological literacy.

CARA is designed so that elderly users never need to interact with the robot for any safety-critical function. All monitoring, fall detection, and alerting happens passively and autonomously.

2.9 — Research Gap and CARA's Positioning

Analysis of existing assistive technologies reveals a critical gap: solutions either address individual aspects of eldercare or are too expensive and connectivity-dependent for deployment in developing countries. No single platform combines affordability, GSM independence, AI fall detection, and passive zero-interaction design.

Key Research Gap Areas

Affordability

Cost Barrier

Existing solutions cost $5,000–$100,000+. CARA targets under $500 total component cost — accessible for families in developing countries.

Connectivity

Wi-Fi Independence

All existing social robots require stable internet. CARA operates entirely on GSM cellular — available in rural Sri Lanka where broadband is not.

Interaction

Zero-Interaction Design

Most systems require elderly users to press buttons, speak, or actively engage. CARA monitors and alerts with zero required elderly interaction.

Integration

Feature Integration

Existing solutions address one or two eldercare needs. CARA integrates 15 features in a single platform: navigation, fall detection, medication, HRI.

Context

Developing World Focus

No existing platform explicitly targets developing-world deployment constraints. CARA's design is driven by Sri Lanka's specific infrastructure realities.

Safety

AI Fall Detection + GSM

Combining computer vision fall detection (MediaPipe) with GSM alerting (no internet) is a novel integration not found in any existing low-cost platform.
