12 Jun 2026 Week 01 Planning Research

Project Kick-Off — Requirements, Literature, and the Research Gap

First week of the CARA thesis project. Reviewed 53 sources, defined five research objectives, and confirmed the core gap: no affordable, Wi-Fi-independent, passive eldercare robot currently exists for developing countries.

Starting Point

Week 1 began with the proposal form already submitted to Supervisor and a broad question in mind: can a single low-cost robot meaningfully address the safety and companionship needs of an elderly person living alone in a developing-country context — specifically Sri Lanka?

The first task was to establish whether this was a genuine gap or whether existing technology already solved it. That meant a structured literature review.

The Literature Review

I reviewed 53 sources across five thematic areas:

  • Ageing demographics — Sri Lanka's demographic transition, old-age dependency ratio trends, implications of working-age migration
  • Assistive robotics — existing platforms (PARO, Pepper, Care-O-Bot 4, ElliQ, Stevie II) and their limitations
  • Fall detection methods — wearable accelerometers, vision-based pose estimation, floor pressure sensors
  • GSM and IoT alerting — SMS-based health monitoring systems operating without internet
  • Human-Robot Interaction with elderly — acceptance factors, zero-interaction design, non-verbal cues

Key finding: Every existing assistive robot either requires Wi-Fi, costs over $5,000, or needs active engagement from the elderly user. None of them are designed for developing-world connectivity constraints. This is the research gap CARA addresses.

Defining the Five Objectives

Based on the literature, five research objectives were drafted and agreed with the supervisor:

ObjectiveWhat it means for the design
OBJ1 — Low-cost mobile platformTotal BOM under $500 · autonomous navigation · person following
OBJ2 — AI fall detectionMediaPipe Pose on Jetson Nano · ≥80% accuracy · <2 false positives/hr
OBJ3 — GSM alertingSIM7600 serious SMS within 30s of fall · no Wi-Fi required
OBJ4 — Medication managementDS3231 RTC · RFID confirm · 15-min missed-dose SMS
OBJ5 — Structured evaluation15 features tested against defined FR pass/fail criteria

Key Design Decisions Made This Week

Several foundational decisions emerged from reviewing the literature and constraints:

GSM over Wi-Fi for all safety-critical communication. This is not a fall-back — it is a deliberate first choice. Wi-Fi is intentionally excluded from safety-critical paths. If it was included, the system would fail in exactly the environments it is designed for.

Zero-interaction design as a non-functional requirement. The literature on HRI with elderly users is consistent: acceptance is highest when the robot does not require the user to learn new behaviour. CARA should monitor and alert without requiring any action from the elderly person.

MediaPipe over wearable accelerometers. Wearables require daily compliance — a problem with elderly users who may forget to wear or charge the device. Vision-based detection requires nothing from the user beyond being in the room.

What Did Not Go Well

The initial scope was too broad. Early drafts of the objectives included more than 20 features. After discussion with the supervisor and research about the use cases and scope deeply, this was reduced to 15 features across three priority tiers — enough to produce meaningful results within the 12-week timeline without overextending the implementation phase.

Lesson: Define evaluation criteria before finalising the feature list. It is much easier to scope out features early than to cut them from an implementation that is already underway.
