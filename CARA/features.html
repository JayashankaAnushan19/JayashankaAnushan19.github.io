15 Integrated Features

CARA integrates 15 capabilities across three priority tiers. Critical features are evaluated first and are non-negotiable for the thesis; high and medium priority features are implemented where hardware and time allow.

Priority tiers: Critical — core safety High — strongly required Medium — desired if time allows

Critical Priority

These features are the core purpose of CARA. Without them, the fundamental research objectives cannot be met. They are implemented and tested first.

FeaturePriorityDescription
🤸 AI Fall Detection Critical MediaPipe Pose extracts 33 body landmarks per frame, from which 69 features are derived and scored by a trained RandomForestClassifier (200 estimators). A two-layer confirmation — a 0.35 confidence entry threshold, then 3 consecutive rising-angle/hip checks over ~2.7s — filters out momentary pose misreads before a fall is confirmed. Evaluated at 87.6% test accuracy, 89.6% cross-validated (see Docs).
📱 GSM Fall Alert SMS Critical Confirmed fall event triggers the GSM module (A7670C; SIM800L in a Sri Lanka deployment) to send SMS to registered family members within 30 seconds. Uses AT command set over the Arduino's hardware Serial1 (not SoftwareSerial) — no internet required. Includes 30-second cooldown to prevent SMS flooding. Status: two-way SMS round-trip confirmed on a bench setup (breadboard + Arduino); the module has not yet responded once wired into the assembled robot and needs its own dedicated power supply — not yet confirmed working end-to-end on the real chassis.
🚗 Obstacle Avoidance Navigation Critical 6 × HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors provide 360° coverage. Emergency stop at 20 cm threshold — non-overridable even during remote drive commands. The fundamental safety invariant of the platform. Status: logic-complete and bench-validated; formal collision-free trial statistics against the 80% target have not yet been completed, pending the drive-motor replacement (see Testing & Evaluation). Terrain limitation: flat, level indoor floors only — the wheeled chassis has no mechanism for climbing or descending stairs, curbs, or steep inclines. This is a hardware limitation of the platform, not a software gap to be tuned away.
👤 Person Following Critical Face recognition (LBPH) is the primary identity lock; a colour-band HSV fallback takes over when the face can't be reliably seen, with band-anchored re-acquisition of the face once it's visible again. Confirmed live on real hardware across two lighting conditions. The keypad is used for enrolment and medication acknowledgement; RFID logs tag scans but no longer confirms anything (see Medication Reminders). Publishes TARGET_LOST if neither the face nor the band is held. The HSV band fallback is reliable only at close range (~50cm; see Testing & Evaluation).
💊 Medication Reminders Critical Design target: DS3231 RTC triggers audible buzzer + LCD message at 08:00, 13:00, 18:00, and 22:00 daily, confirmed by keypad acknowledge. Status: keypad acknowledgement works, now with an escalating alert (6 messages 5 minutes apart, then buzzer/LCD held active) rather than a single notification. RFID acknowledgement was implemented, then removed after testing found it unreliable specifically when the Mega is powered through the Jetson's USB port — a real power-quality fault, not a wiring issue (see Blog Week 16). The DS3231 RTC is currently disabled after a hardware fault, so live time-of-day scheduling isn't running yet — assessed as partially achieved.
⚠️ Missed-Dose SMS Alert Critical Design target: if medication confirmation is not received within 15 minutes of a reminder trigger, the Arduino automatically calls the GSM SMS function to alert registered family members. Status: the escalating alert mechanism itself works (keypad-acknowledged, GSM module confirmed functional with its voltage requirement understood); the full chain hasn't been confirmed end-to-end on the assembled robot yet, since it also depends on the RTC-based scheduling above.

High Priority

These features significantly extend CARA's usefulness and are planned for full implementation alongside the critical tier.

FeaturePriorityDescription
📷 Live Camera Feed (Phone Browser) High Flask MJPEG streaming server runs on Jetson Nano port 5000. Family members access cara.local:5000/video_feed from any phone browser on the local network — no app required.
🕹️ Remote Drive (Phone Browser) High HTTP POST endpoint receives directional commands from a phone web interface. Motor commands are forwarded to Arduino via serial — but obstacle avoidance always takes precedence, regardless of remote command.
🌡️ Temperature Monitoring & Alert Postponed → v1.1 High DS18B20 sensor would monitor room temperature and, if ambient temperature exceeds 38°C for more than 5 minutes, trigger a heatstroke-risk SMS alert and LCD warning message. Proposed for v1.0 but postponed to v1.1 due to time constraints — not implemented in the current build.
🔋 Battery Management & Low-Battery SMS Alert Postponed → v2.0 High Designed behaviour: a voltage divider monitors the LiPo pack, switching the status LED to a rapid blink pattern and showing a low-battery warning on the LCD below ≈25% capacity, then sending an SMS asking a family member to replace the battery once it's critically low or dead. None of this is implemented in the current build — not the voltage monitoring, not the SMS. Planned for v2.0, not v1.0/v1.1.
📅 Daily Check-In SMS High At 12:00 noon each day, the health_management ROS node sends a status SMS confirming that CARA is online and the user was seen in the past 4 hours. Families receive silence-is-an-alert assurance.
🎙️ Greeting & LCD Messages High When person presence is detected via PIR sensor, CARA displays a warm greeting on the 16×2 I2C LCD and activates a short audible chime. Messages rotate between time-appropriate greetings based on RTC hour.

Medium Priority

These features enhance the interaction quality and data collection capabilities. They will be implemented if the core tiers are stable within the 12-week timeline.

FeaturePriorityDescription
💡 LED State Feedback Medium A single status LED (pin 10, PWM-dimmable) provides visual status: gentle brightness pulse in IDLE, blink pattern during ALERT. Simplified down from an earlier 3× RGB LED design — colour-coded states were not carried into the final build.
🔻 Cliff / Stair Detection Postponed → v1.1 Medium Three TCRT5000 infrared sensors mounted facing downward detect floor edge loss (staircase or dropped surface). Emergency stop is triggered if two or more sensors simultaneously lose floor reflection. Proposed for v1.0 but postponed to v1.1 due to time constraints — not implemented in the current build.
👋 Greeting Wave Gesture Medium One MG90S servo (pin 46) performs a one-shot wave gesture on greeting — not a continuous camera-tilt or tracking mechanism. There is no pan-tilt hardware on this robot; camera aiming (left-right) is done by rotating the chassis itself, and there is no vertical/tilt axis at all.

Feature Implementation Status

Features are being implemented sequentially from Critical → High → Medium throughout Weeks 3–8 of the project. Current implementation status is logged in the Development Blog.
