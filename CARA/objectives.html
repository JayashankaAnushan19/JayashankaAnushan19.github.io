Research Framework

Objectives & Research Questions

Five interconnected research objectives define what CARA must achieve. Each objective is paired with a corresponding research question that the evaluation will answer.

Research Objectives

These five objectives were defined at the outset of the project to give the design and evaluation a clear direction. They form the basis of the functional requirements, the feature set, and the testing protocol.

01

Design and build a low-cost mobile robot platform

Design and build a mobile robot platform with autonomous navigation, obstacle avoidance, and person-following capabilities — targeting a total component cost under $500 USD to ensure accessibility for families in developing countries.

02

Implement AI-based fall detection

Implement a computer vision fall detection system using MediaPipe Pose Estimation on NVIDIA Jetson Nano, achieving at least 80% detection accuracy across 20 standardised trials while minimising false positives to under 2 per hour of continuous operation.

03

Develop a GSM-based emergency alerting system

Develop a GSM-based emergency alerting system using the A7670C (SIM800L for a Sri Lanka deployment, where 2G is still active) that delivers SMS notifications to registered family members within 30 seconds of a confirmed fall event — operating without any Wi-Fi or internet connectivity.

04

Create an automated medication management system

Create an automated medication reminder and confirmation system using DS3231 RTC and MFRC522 RFID, capable of triggering scheduled reminders at four daily intervals and sending missed-dose alerts via GSM within 15 minutes of a non-confirmed dose.

05

Evaluate performance against defined metrics

Evaluate CARA's performance against all 15 defined functional requirements through structured lab trials, measuring fall detection accuracy, SMS delivery time, navigation success rate, medication reminder precision, and overall system reliability.

Research Questions

The following research questions frame the inquiry. They are designed to be answerable through structured experimentation at the end of the project, providing the evaluative basis for the thesis conclusion.

RQ1

How effectively can a sub-$500 robot platform navigate and follow a user in a typical home environment?

Measured by: percentage of navigation trials completed without collision; percentage of person-following sessions where the target remains within camera frame for more than 80% of session duration.

RQ2

Can MediaPipe Pose Estimation on a Jetson Nano reliably detect falls in standardised trials?

Measured by: true positive rate (sensitivity) and false positive rate across 20 standardised fall simulation trials in varied room positions and lighting conditions.

RQ3

Can GSM-based SMS alerting consistently deliver notifications within 30 seconds without internet access?

Measured by: average and maximum SMS delivery time across 15 triggered alert tests, conducted without Wi-Fi connectivity, in a lab environment with GSM coverage.

RQ4

How reliably can the medication management system trigger scheduled reminders and detect confirmation?

Measured by: percentage of scheduled reminders triggered at the correct time (±1 minute); RFID confirmation detection rate across 20 trials; SMS alert delivery rate for missed doses.

RQ5

Can CARA achieve 80% or greater pass rates across all 15 defined functional requirements in structured evaluation?

This is the overarching question synthesising all five objectives. The final evaluation chapter will report pass/fail results for each feature and an overall system effectiveness score.
