Objectives

What V2.0 is trying to achieve, the constraints that don’t change, and the experiment that will show whether each goal was met.

Design constraints (unchanged from V1)

  • Cost: component budget under $500. The V1 prototype came to $409.10.
  • No Wi-Fi dependence for safety: emergency alerts must go out over the mobile network even if the home internet is down.
  • Safety logic survives an AI crash: alerting, the panic button and obstacle stops run on the microcontroller, independent of the AI computer.
  • Usable by a non-technical person: a keypad, a card tap and a phone browser — no computer needed.

V2.0 objectives

O1 · A reproducible platform

The repository rebuilds exactly the robot being tested: firmware, software and a verified pin map, tagged as a baseline.

O2 · Dependable alerting

SMS alerts delivered within 30 s of a confirmed event, measured over repeated timed trials, with the supply margin the GSM module needs written down as a design rule.

O3 · Fall detection measured where it matters

Accuracy, false alarms per hour and fall-to-alert time measured on the robot itself, across lighting and occupancy — not only on offline images.

EXP-04

O4 · Follow the right person

Keep the enrolled person framed at a comfortable distance with visitors present, and turn reliably to do so.

EXP-05EXP-06

O5 · Reach the family abroad securely

Remote access to the dashboard over the internet, protected by proper authentication, with the family told by SMS whenever the link changes.

EXP-07

O6 · One system that runs all day

All features running together for 24 hours without device conflicts or restarts.

EXP-08

O7 · Publishable knowledge

Turn results — including the fault-finding method — into journal entries that can become research papers.

Out of scope for now

  • Speech interaction.
  • Trials with older adults outside the developer’s own home — these would need proper ethical safeguards first.
